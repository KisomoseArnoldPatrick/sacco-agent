# Loan Default and Recovery

This document describes what happens after a loan is classified as in
default, as defined in `repayment_policy.md` (repayments overdue by more
than 60 days).

## Step 1 — Default notice

A written default notice is sent to the member and to each of their
guarantors as soon as a loan is classified as in default.

## Step 2 — Credit Committee review

The Credit Committee reviews the defaulted case within **14 days** of the
default classification, and may:

- Agree a recovery plan directly with the member, or
- Proceed to guarantor liability if no agreement is reached.

## Step 3 — Guarantor liability

If the default remains unresolved **30 days** after the default notice was
sent, the guarantor(s) on the loan become liable to cover the outstanding
balance from their own savings and share capital, consistent with the
guarantor consent form they signed at application (see
`guarantor_requirements.md`).

## Step 4 — Escalation

Cases that remain unresolved after guarantor liability has been applied are
forwarded to the Board of Directors, who may authorize further recovery
action, including legal recovery, as a last resort.

## Decision authority

- Loan officers cannot authorize any step in this process.
- Only the Credit Committee and, at the final escalation stage, the Board
  of Directors, may make default-recovery decisions (see
  `sacco_governance_and_committees.md`).
